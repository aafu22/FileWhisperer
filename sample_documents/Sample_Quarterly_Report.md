# Northwind Trading Co. — Q3 2026 Business Review

## Company overview

Northwind Trading Co. is a mid-sized outdoor equipment retailer operating
across 14 stores in the Pacific Northwest, plus an online storefront
launched in 2024. The company has 210 employees and is privately held.

## Financial highlights

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.1M | $3.6M | $4.2M |
| Gross margin | 41% | 43% | 45% |
| Online share of sales | 18% | 22% | 27% |
| New store openings | 0 | 1 | 2 |

Q3 revenue of $4.2M represents 17% growth over Q2, driven primarily by
the online storefront, which grew from 22% to 27% of total sales. The
two new store openings (Bend, OR and Boise, ID) contributed a combined
$310K in Q3 revenue, ahead of the $250K opening-quarter target set for
each location.

## Risks and open items

1. **Vendor lead times**: The company's primary tent and backpack
   supplier has extended lead times from 6 to 11 weeks, creating risk for
   the spring 2027 inventory cycle. Procurement is evaluating a second
   supplier based in Vietnam as a hedge.
2. **Warehouse capacity**: The Tacoma distribution center is projected to
   reach 90% capacity by Q1 2027 at the current growth rate. A capacity
   study is due from Operations by the end of Q4.
3. **Seasonal staffing**: Q4 holiday hiring is tracking 15% behind last
   year's pace in the Portland market specifically, attributed to
   competing seasonal wages at two large warehouses that opened nearby.

## Action items

- Procurement to complete supplier evaluation and report back by
  November 1
- Operations to deliver the Tacoma warehouse capacity study by December 1
- Portland store managers to raise seasonal wage offers by $1.50/hr,
  effective immediately, pending regional HR sign-off

## Customer feedback summary

Net Promoter Score held steady at 62 (up 1 point from Q2). The most
common positive theme in open-text feedback was in-store staff knowledge;
the most common negative theme was checkout wait times during weekend
afternoons at the three highest-traffic locations (Seattle-Downtown,
Portland-Pearl, and Bend).
